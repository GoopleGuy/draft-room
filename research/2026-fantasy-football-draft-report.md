# 2026 Fantasy Football Draft Report
### Positional Top 15s, Sleepers, and Draft Blueprints
**Compiled August 6, 2026** — reflecting training camp news through this morning

## How to Read This Report

**Baseline format assumed:** 12-team, full PPR, starting QB/RB/RB/WR/WR/WR/TE/FLEX/K/DST, 6 bench. Adjustments for half-PPR and superflex are flagged inline.

**Ranking methodology.** The spine of each list is the Rotoworld Top 200 (updated August 3, 2026), which is an average of the ranks of Kyle Dvorchak, Denny Carter, and Patrick Daugherty. I then cross-checked every player against PFF, ESPN, CBS Sports, Yahoo, Sports Illustrated, RotoWire, FTN, Fantasy Life, and Footballguys, and moved players where there was broad disagreement with Rotoworld. Where credible outlets genuinely disagree, I say so rather than hiding it behind a single number — that disagreement *is* the actionable information, because it tells you where the market is soft.

**A candid note on confidence.** Rankings in early August are perishable. Three of the players below have unresolved health situations that could move them a full tier in either direction by Labor Day (Nabers, Kittle, Alec Pierce). Treat this as a decision framework, not a script.

**One data-quality flag worth knowing about:** the Rotoworld board still lists A.J. Brown with Philadelphia and Deebo Samuel as a free agent. Both are stale labels — Brown was traded to New England on June 1, and Samuel signed with San Francisco after Ricky Pearsall's season-ending surgery. I've corrected both below. If your league's default rankings are pulled from a stale feed, check team labels before you draft.

## Executive Summary — The Five Things That Define This Draft

1. **Running back is king again at the top.** The first two picks are running backs by consensus, and five of the top eight players on most boards are RBs. Michael Fabiano's top 200 notes that 11 of the top 20 players in his rankings are backs.
2. **The elite WR tier is only four deep.** Ja'Marr Chase, Puka Nacua, Jaxon Smith-Njigba, and Amon-Ra St. Brown are separated from the field by a real gap. Heath Cummings (CBS) projects all four above 18 PPR points per game and nobody else in that neighborhood.
3. **Quarterback is the deepest it has ever been — so wait.** PFF's position summary is blunt: even Tier 4 quarterbacks carry weekly starting value in single-QB leagues. The value is in Tier 2 (Daniels, Burrow, Maye, Hurts), not Tier 1.
4. **Tight end has a genuine top six for the first time in years.** Bowers, McBride, Loveland, Warren, Fannin, and Kraft all have TE1-overall paths. That means you can either pay up early or comfortably wait — but the dead zone after Kraft is severe.
5. **The 2026 rookie RB class is thin, which inflates two names.** Jeremiyah Love and Jadarian Price are essentially the entire rookie RB market, and their ADPs reflect scarcity as much as projection.

### QB Sleepers

- **Tyler Shough (NO)** — ESPN's Liz Loza's headline late-round flier. Kellen Moore's pass-heavy Saints offense, rookie Jordyn Tyson arriving, and 10 indoor games. *Source: ESPN late-round fliers; SI rookie rankings.*
- **Kyler Murray (MIN)** — Released by Arizona, signed a one-year deal with Minnesota to compete with J.J. McCarthy. The reason to care: **the Vikings have by far the easiest fantasy-playoff schedule (Weeks 14–17)** per Rotoworld's opponent-EPA analysis. He's been falling to Round 13 in mocks. *Source: Rotoworld QB Tiers; RotoWire mock draft; commishkit offseason tracker.*
- **Bo Nix (DEN)** — Jaylen Waddle joins him in Denver. Solid QB2 with a real weapon upgrade. *Source: CBS WR tiers; commishkit.*
- **Malik Willis (MIA)** — Rushing upside in a starting job. FantasyPros calls him "a wild card in the 2026 QB picture." Deep-league dart only. *Source: FantasyPros 1QB tiers.*

> **Strategic read:** The correct QB play in 1QB leagues is to let Allen and Jackson go, then take Daniels, Burrow, Maye, or Hurts three-plus rounds later. If you land a Tier 4 QB instead (Dart, Love, Purdy, Stafford, Lawrence), pair him with a Tier 5 arm on a complementary bye and opposite playoff schedule — Rotoworld specifically notes the Vikings/Colts (easy) vs. 49ers/Buccaneers (hard) split, and that none of those teams share a bye.

### RB Sleepers — the section that wins leagues

- **Blake Corum (LAR)** — *The best late-round RB on the board.* From Week 13 on in 2025 he averaged 10.5 carries, 67.5 rushing yards, and 12.7 PPR points per game with five TDs. Denny Carter: conservatively a top-10 fantasy option if Kyren misses time, and "nothing but bad touchdown luck" would stop him from being a weekly top-five back in that scenario. Rare combination of standalone weekly value *and* extreme contingency value. *Sources: Rotoworld Zero RB; FantasyPros Zero RB.*
- **Kenneth Gainwell (TB)** — ADP around 100. Set career highs across the board in Pittsburgh with 1,023 scrimmage yards. Bucs OC Zac Robinson said in June that Gainwell and Bucky Irving "can kind of match each other" in the pass game — read that as bad for Irving, good for Gainwell. SportsLine's model flags him as a sleeper. *Sources: Rotoworld Zero RB; CBS/SportsLine; Yahoo mock.*
- **Jadarian Price (SEA)** — Drafted 32nd overall. Walker is gone and Zach Charbonnet is recovering from a late-season ACL tear; between them Seattle ran the ball **405 times** last season and both averaged 11.3 FPPG. That's a top-10 workload available to a first-round rookie. Risks: new OC after Klint Kubiak left for Las Vegas, and possible Super Bowl hangover. *Sources: RotoBaller; DraftSharks; Footballguys.*
- **Jonathon Brooks (CAR)** — The biggest riser on CBS's rank-movement report this week. Two ACL tears in the same knee in 13 months, but he opened camp without issue, is cutting and catching well, and came in stronger. Carolina let Rico Dowdle walk and ignored RB in the draft. ADP ~125. *Sources: CBS risers/fallers (Aug 6); ESPN sleepers.*
- **Tank Bigsby (PHI)** — Pure contingency: Fantasy Life expects no value with Barkley healthy, but arguably the highest ceiling on their handcuff list if Barkley misses games. Posted 5.7 YPC in 2025, the best per-touch season of his career. *Sources: Rotoworld Zero RB; Fantasy Life handcuffs.*
- **Rachaad White (WAS)** — Could win the starting job outright out of camp. Volume-based dice roll. *Source: FantasyPros risers/fallers.*
- **Kyle Monangai / D'Andre Swift (CHI)** — Fantasy Life's point: Bears hype is all on the passing game, but Ben Johnson runs a balanced offense. Whichever of these two consolidates touches has a high-end RB2 floor. *Source: Fantasy Life handcuffs.*

# DEFENSE / SPECIAL TEAMS — Top 14

FantasyPros' guidance is worth repeating verbatim in spirit: **if your league doesn't require you to draft one, don't.** You will find a usable DST on waivers. # MOCK DRAFT #3 — Zero-RB Skeleton, Pick 6 of 12
Denny Carter's disclaimer applies and it matters: **Zero RB is not for every league.** In a 10-team league starting two wide receivers, don't bother. In a 14-team PPR league with multiple flex spots, it works fine. In standard (non-PPR) scoring, it makes very little sense.

**Rounds 1–5 (all non-RB):** Jaxon Smith-Njigba (1.06) → Nico Collins (2.07) → Brock Bowers (3.06) → Zay Flowers (4.07) → Drake Maye (5.06)
**Rounds 6–9 (break the seal):** Blake Corum → Jadarian Price → Kenneth Gainwell → Rachaad White
**Rounds 10–13 (volume at RB):** Jonathon Brooks → Tank Bigsby → Jacory Croskey-Merritt → Chris Rodriguez Jr.
**Rounds 14–15:** K, DST

The thesis is explicit: you are buying six or seven lottery tickets and betting that two of them convert into every-week starters by mid-season. Fantasy Life's writeup after running this exercise on Underdog was that they came out **more** bullish on Zero RB than they went in — but graded the resulting roster a B, not an A. That's the honest expectation.

# WHAT TO MONITOR BETWEEN NOW AND YOUR DRAFT

These are the five situations most likely to change the board in the next three weeks:

1. **Malik Nabers' ramp-up (NYG).** He's practicing and feeling better than expected, and his ADP moved +10 in a week. If he takes full-team reps in the preseason, he belongs in the top 20 overall. If there's a setback, he falls out of Round 3.
2. **Alec Pierce's ankle (IND).** Ballard expects him back in the first half of August. Watch for actual practice participation, not team optimism.
3. **George Kittle's Achilles (SF).** He's tracking for Week 1, but Rotoworld's caution is the right frame: you can't evaluate an older player's Achilles recovery until you watch him move. His price should tell you whether the market has priced the risk.
4. **The Atlanta quarterback situation.** Tua is slated to start Week 1 while nursing a knee injury, and Penix is also hurt. Drake London and Kyle Pitts are both priced with uncertainty, and either could become a bargain or a trap.
5. **The New England backfield.** If Henderson keeps taking second-team reps, Rhamondre Stevenson becomes a genuine value in Round 8 and Henderson becomes a Round 5 trap.

**A general caution:** the DST and kicker landscape is the least predictable part of any draft. SI's own retrospective makes the point well — Denver went from 20th to 1st in a single season while highly drafted Dallas, San Francisco, and Cleveland units all finished 14th or worse. Spend your final picks accordingly, which is to say: cheaply.
